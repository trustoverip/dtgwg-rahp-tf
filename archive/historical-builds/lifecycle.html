Lifecycle & gaps

The RAHP method across the five stages of standards development, with every known gap as a tracked record.

generated by tools/build.py from data/ · 43 risk · 66 control · 21 guardrail · 21 assurance_test · 37 metric · 36 user_story · 33 scenario · 21 epic · 16 persona · 9 recommendation · 3 risk_acceptance · 3 governance_precedent

Core principle. People and implementation context drive everything. Every risk score, control, guardrail, assurance test, trust metric and user story is only as analytically sound as the Context stage that preceded it. The toolkit is not a framework applied to a system — it is derived from a community.

STAGE-1 · Context & scoping

The generative engine. Community inquiry, extreme user and bad-actor identification, persona synthesis, and context validation.
Input artefacts
Working group charter, Problem statement, Technical scope, Threat intelligence
Produces
persona
Required outputs
Raw participant map; Implementation context document; Extreme user profiles; Bad-actor persona drafts; Validated context baseline and objectives map
Evidence required
Signed-off persona register with named contributor and source material per persona
GAP-1.1 — No defined standard for how much community inquiry is sufficient before Phase B can begin.
GAP-1.2 — No protocol for representing communities who cannot participate directly (children, people in high-risk political contexts, future users). The Verifiable AI SDJ methodology (Hickman, 2025) is a partial approach, not yet integrated into the workflow.

STAGE-2 · Drafting

Continuous risk-design-control loop. Every significant design choice is proposed, assessed, controlled and documented before acceptance into the draft.
Input artefacts
Validated persona set, Risk register, Draft specification text
Produces
risk, control, guardrail, assurance_test, metric, user_story, scenario
Required outputs
Risk-control mapping with rationale; Provenance-tagged toolkit additions
Evidence required
For each accepted design choice — the affected risks, the selected controls, the metric linkage, and the provenance tag naming the design decision and specification version that triggered it.
GAP-2.1 — No defined protocol for what constitutes adequate control coverage before a design choice is accepted into the draft.
GAP-2.2 — No agreed threshold for when a newly surfaced risk requires a full persona update versus a note in the register.

STAGE-3 · Review & harmonisation

Three parallel tracks — cross-standard alignment, community review, formal risk acceptance — converging at a harmonisation point before publication.
Input artefacts
Draft specification, Risk register, Adjacent standards
Produces
recommendation, risk_acceptance, spec_review
Required outputs
Specification gap analysis; Updated risk/control/guardrail/assurance records; REC records
Evidence required
Specification commentary report; every risk either adequately controlled or formally accepted with named authority and review date
GAP-3.1 — Formal risk acceptance workflow not yet defined — who can accept a risk, under what authority, for how long, with what documentation. The most significant process gap in the toolkit.
addressed by data/risk-acceptances.yaml (schema exists; authority model still undecided)
GAP-3.2 — No defined process for community outreach during review; passive invitation is insufficient.
GAP-3.3 — No protocol for translating community feedback not expressed in standards language into artefact updates.

STAGE-4 · Publication

Point-in-time event with ongoing implications. The toolkit snapshot is version-bound to the standard version and transitions from development tool to implementation support tool.
Input artefacts
Validated artefact set
Produces
conformance_claim
Required outputs
Normative candidate list; Assurance test suite; Tagged release; CHANGELOG entry
Evidence required
Conformance claim template; bidirectional version binding between toolkit version and specification version
GAP-4.1 — No implementation guidance document bridging toolkit artefacts and common deployment patterns.
GAP-4.2 — No structured intake process for collecting implementation experience from early adopters.

STAGE-5 · Maintenance

The longest and most underserved stage. Three trigger streams feed a triage and integration process on an annual review cadence, with continuous monitoring between reviews.
Input artefacts
Field reports, Threat model review, Specification revisions
Produces
risk, governance_precedent, changelog_entry
Required outputs
Updated records; New risks; Diff-annotated changelog entry
Evidence required
Dated review record covering all three trigger streams and the periodic extreme user review
GAP-5.1 — Contribution and integration governance not defined — no triage process, no acceptance authority, no decision on what enters the next toolkit version.
GAP-5.2 — No structured intake process for operational experience reports from implementors.
GAP-5.3 — No monitoring process specified for threat landscape tracking between annual reviews.
GAP-5.4 — Governance precedent records not yet in use — decisions are not retained between governance sessions.
addressed by data/governance-precedents.yaml
GAP-5.5 — Feedback loop design deliberately deferred pending practitioner trials. The loop must be empirically grounded, not designed in theory and imposed on practitioners.
