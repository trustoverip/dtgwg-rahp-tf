Risk Assessment and Harms Prevention

Find out who could be harmed by a digital trust system, and where the fix belongs.

RAHP is a method, a catalogue of risks and a small set of checks. It helps people review specifications and the systems built from them through the eyes of the people who will live with them. People take part from start to finish, not only at one checkpoint. AI can read and draft at scale, and machines pin and check, but some steps are for people by rule.

The journey

Eighteen steps, and who does each one

People frame, choose, judge, decide and live with the result. AI drafts and reads at scale. Machines pin, check and fingerprint. Four steps are for people by rule, whatever tools you use.

  • Person: decides, judges or acts
  • Person, by rule: AI must not do this
  • AI: drafts, reads or proposes; a person reviews it
  • Machine: a script or check that gives the same answer every time

    Rules for using AI

    • AI assists. People decide.
    • Don't use AI to score risks, verify must-haves (guardrails) or make governance decisions.
    • Check every quotation and citation against the source. Then check the judgement, not only the quote.
    • Have a second person, or a different model, check independently.

    Label what AI drafted

    An AI-drafted record says "AI draft, not yet reviewed by" and a name, until that person has reviewed the findings. A record never names someone as its reviewer before they have reviewed it.

    Without AI takes longer. Reading one specification can take a day or more. Split several specifications across people, then meet to find the themes.

    3. Hear whether each point was accepted, declined or deferred.

    Share a finding safely

    No code

    For anyone with evidence of harm, including people who can't be named.

    1. Choose a route: public, through a form, or through a trusted intermediary.
    2. Say what happens, to whom, and where you saw it.
    3. See the outcome and the reason.

    Review a specification

    Some setup

    For specification editors and reviewers.

    1. Fix the exact version you are reading.
    2. Read it with a person's eyes, noting the section for each concern.
    3. Link each finding to a risk, and say where the fix belongs.

    Joins the journey atSteps 1 to 12, then hand over for examination

    You need: the specification's repository. Python only for the optional check.

    Review an implementation

    Technical

    For implementers and testers, including several specifications working together.

    1. Take guardrails as short, testable statements.
    2. Turn them into checks in your own pipeline.
    3. Record what the system does for real people, not just what it claims.

    Joins the journey atSteps 1 to 12, and step 17 when the code changes

    You need: Git and Python 3.

    Some setup

    For working group chairs and new group leads.

    1. Keep the method; replace the catalogue with your own people and risks.
    2. Start from a small set of defaults.
    3. Agree how much risk your group will accept, and who decides.

    Terms and codes used in the files

    People are personas; situations are scenarios; safeguards are controls; must-haves are guardrails (a stage cannot pass without them); measures are metrics and assurance tests. In the files they carry codes such as RK- for risks and CT- for controls.
